# Chikungunya

## Overview
Chikungunya is a viral disease caused by the chikungunya virus (CHIKV). It is spread to people by the bites of infected Aedes mosquitoes, mainly Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus, the same mosquitoes that spread dengue. The name comes from a word in the Kimakonde language meaning "to become contorted", describing the stooped posture caused by joint pain.

Chikungunya is rarely fatal, but the joint pain can be severe and disabling, and in some people it lasts for months or longer.

## Symptoms
Symptoms usually begin 4 to 8 days after the bite of an infected mosquito (range 2 to 12 days). The most common symptoms are:
- sudden high fever
- severe joint pain, often in the hands, wrists and ankles, usually on both sides of the body
- joint swelling
- muscle pain
- headache
- nausea and tiredness
- skin rash

Most people feel better within a week, but joint pain can persist for months.

## Who is at higher risk
Severe illness is more likely in:
- newborns infected around the time of birth
- older adults, especially those over 65
- people with long-term conditions such as diabetes, heart disease or high blood pressure

Rare complications can affect the eyes, nerves, brain and heart. Seek urgent care for a newborn with fever, or for anyone with confusion, seizures, difficulty breathing, inability to keep fluids down, or very little urine.

## Chikungunya and dengue
Chikungunya and dengue are caused by different viruses but are spread by the same mosquitoes and cause similar early symptoms such as fever, rash and body pain. Severe joint pain is more typical of chikungunya. Because dengue can become severe, a doctor should rule out dengue with blood tests. A person can be infected with both at the same time.

## Transmission
Chikungunya spreads through the bite of an infected Aedes mosquito. These mosquitoes bite mainly during the daytime and breed in clean, stagnant water in containers around homes. Chikungunya does not spread directly from person to person through touch, coughing or sharing food. Rarely, it can pass from a mother to her newborn around the time of birth.

## Diagnosis and care
Chikungunya is confirmed by blood tests ordered by a doctor: a PCR test in the first week of illness, or antibody tests later.

There is no specific antiviral treatment. Care focuses on rest, fluids and relief of fever and pain under a doctor's guidance. Some painkillers, such as aspirin and other anti-inflammatory medicines, should not be taken until dengue has been ruled out, because they can increase bleeding risk in dengue. Do not self-medicate.

Chikungunya vaccines have been approved in some countries for specific groups of people. Ask a doctor about availability and whether vaccination is suitable.

## Prevention
Prevention is the same as for dengue:
- empty, scrub and cover water containers, coolers and flower-pot trays every week
- remove discarded tyres, cans and plastic that collect rainwater
- wear long-sleeved clothing and use mosquito repellent, especially during daytime
- use window and door screens and bed nets
- a person with chikungunya should avoid mosquito bites during the first week of illness so the virus is not spread further

## Common misconceptions
- "Chikungunya and dengue are the same disease." False. They are caused by different viruses, although the same mosquitoes spread both.
- "Chikungunya spreads from person to person." False. It spreads through mosquito bites.
- "Aedes mosquitoes bite only at night." False. They bite mainly during the day.
- "Chikungunya always causes permanent joint damage." False. Most people recover fully, although joint pain lasts for months in some.

## When to seek care
See a doctor for a sudden fever with joint pain, so that dengue can be ruled out. Seek urgent care for any of the danger signs listed above.
