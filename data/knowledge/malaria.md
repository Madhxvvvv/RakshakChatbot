# Malaria

## Overview
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites, which are spread to people through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Five Plasmodium species cause malaria in humans; Plasmodium falciparum and Plasmodium vivax are the most common and both occur in India. Malaria is preventable and curable.

Falciparum malaria can progress to severe illness and death within 24 hours if not treated, which is why prompt testing matters.

## Symptoms
Symptoms usually appear 10 to 15 days after the infective mosquito bite. The first symptoms are often fever, chills and headache, and may be mild and hard to recognise as malaria. Other symptoms include:
- sweating
- muscle aches and tiredness
- nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea

Fever may come and go in cycles. Plasmodium vivax and Plasmodium ovale can stay dormant in the liver and cause relapses weeks or months later.

Malaria cannot be told apart from dengue, typhoid or chikungunya by symptoms alone; a blood test is needed.

## Warning signs of severe malaria
Severe malaria is a medical emergency. Seek hospital care immediately for:
- extreme tiredness, drowsiness or confusion
- impaired consciousness or unconsciousness
- convulsions (fits)
- difficulty breathing
- dark or bloody urine
- yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)
- abnormal bleeding
- inability to drink, eat or sit up

Young children, pregnant women, people living with HIV, and travellers from areas without malaria are at higher risk of severe disease.

## Transmission
Malaria spreads through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. These mosquitoes bite mostly between dusk and dawn. They breed in fresh water such as ponds, puddles, rice fields, wells and water storage containers; one species, Anopheles stephensi, breeds in containers in towns and cities.

Malaria does not spread from person to person through touch, coughing or sharing food. Rarely, it can spread through blood transfusion, organ transplant, shared needles, or from a mother to her baby during pregnancy.

## Diagnosis and care
Malaria must be confirmed with a blood test before treatment: either a blood smear examined under a microscope, or a rapid diagnostic test (RDT). In India, testing and treatment for malaria are available free at government health facilities.

Malaria is curable with effective medicines. A doctor chooses the treatment based on the parasite species and how severe the illness is. Taking the full course exactly as prescribed is important, even after the fever settles, to clear the infection and prevent relapse and drug resistance.

## Prevention
- sleep under insecticide-treated bed nets
- support indoor residual spraying of insecticide in high-risk areas
- wear long-sleeved clothing and use mosquito repellent from dusk to dawn
- use window and door screens
- remove standing water and stagnant puddles near homes
- travellers to high-risk areas should ask a doctor about preventive medicines before the trip

WHO recommends malaria vaccines (RTS,S and R21) for children living in areas with moderate to high transmission of falciparum malaria, mainly in Africa.

## Common misconceptions
- "Malaria spreads from person to person." False. It spreads through mosquito bites.
- "Once the fever is gone, the medicine can be stopped." False. Stopping treatment early can leave parasites in the body, causing relapse and encouraging drug resistance.
- "Every fever is malaria, so treatment can start without a test." False. Fever has many causes; malaria should be confirmed with a blood test.
- "Malaria only occurs in villages." False. Some malaria mosquitoes breed in urban water containers, so city dwellers are also at risk.

## When to seek care
Get tested promptly for any fever if you live in or have travelled to an area with malaria. Seek emergency care immediately for any warning sign of severe malaria.
