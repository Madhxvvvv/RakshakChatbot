# Dengue

## Overview
Dengue is a viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV), which has four types (serotypes). It is spread to people through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, mainly Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. Dengue is common in tropical and subtropical regions, and India sees seasonal outbreaks that peak during and after the monsoon.

Most people with dengue have mild symptoms or none at all, and recover in one to two weeks. A small proportion develop severe dengue, which can be fatal without timely medical care. A second infection with a different dengue type increases the risk of severe dengue.

## Symptoms
Symptoms usually begin 4 to 10 days after the bite of an infected mosquito and last 2 to 7 days. The main symptom is a high fever (around 40°C / 104°F), together with any of the following:
- severe headache
- pain behind the eyes
- muscle and joint pains
- nausea and vomiting
- swollen glands
- skin rash

Dengue cannot be told apart from malaria, typhoid or chikungunya by symptoms alone; a blood test ordered by a doctor is needed.

## Warning signs of severe dengue
Warning signs usually appear 24 to 48 hours after the fever goes away. This is the critical phase, and anyone with these signs needs to go to a hospital immediately:
- severe abdominal (belly) pain
- persistent vomiting
- rapid breathing
- bleeding gums or nose
- blood in vomit or stool
- extreme tiredness, restlessness or irritability
- being very thirsty
- pale, cold or clammy skin
- feeling very weak

Infants, pregnant women, older adults and people with long-term health conditions are at higher risk of severe illness.

## Transmission
Dengue spreads through the bite of an infected female Aedes mosquito. These mosquitoes bite mainly during the daytime, with peaks in the early morning and late afternoon, although they can also bite at night in lit areas. They breed in clean, stagnant water collected in containers such as coolers, flower pots, buckets, discarded tyres and water storage tanks.

Dengue does not spread directly from person to person through touch, coughing or sharing food. Rarely, it can pass from a pregnant mother to her baby, or through blood transfusion or organ donation.

## Diagnosis and care
Dengue is confirmed with blood tests ordered by a doctor, such as the NS1 antigen test, a PCR test, or antibody (IgM) tests. A doctor may also monitor blood counts, including platelets.

There is no specific antiviral treatment for dengue. Care focuses on rest, adequate fluids and careful medical monitoring, especially during the critical phase. Do not self-medicate: some common painkillers, such as aspirin and ibuprofen, can increase the risk of bleeding in dengue, so any medicine should be taken only on a doctor's advice.

Dengue vaccines have been approved in some countries for specific groups of people. Ask a doctor or the local health authority about availability and eligibility.

## Prevention
The most effective protection is to prevent mosquito breeding and avoid bites:
- empty, scrub and refill water containers, coolers and flower-pot trays at least once a week
- cover water storage containers and dispose of discarded tyres, cans and plastic that collect rainwater
- wear long-sleeved clothing and use mosquito repellent, especially during daytime
- use window and door screens, and bed nets for anyone sleeping during the day, such as infants and the sick
- support community clean-up and fogging drives during outbreaks

A person who has dengue should avoid mosquito bites during the first week of illness so that mosquitoes do not pick up the virus and spread it further.

## Common misconceptions
- "Dengue mosquitoes bite only at night." False. Aedes mosquitoes bite mainly during the day, so daytime protection matters.
- "Once the fever goes down, the danger has passed." False. Warning signs of severe dengue usually appear around the time the fever falls, so this is when close watching matters most.
- "Dengue spreads from person to person." False. It spreads through mosquito bites, not through touch, coughing or shared food.
- "Home remedies such as papaya leaf juice cure dengue." There is no proven cure for dengue. Home remedies must never replace medical assessment and monitoring.

## When to seek care
See a doctor if you develop a high fever during the dengue season or after travel to an affected area. Go to a hospital immediately if any warning sign of severe dengue appears.
